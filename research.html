The Impact of U.S. Tariffs and Free Trade Agreements on Bilateral Trade

Chowdhury, M. Datta, and D. Miljkovic

Submitted to The Manchester School, 2026

+24%trade with FTA entry (TWFE)
20U.S. FTA partner countries
2000–2024panel coverage
TWFE + PPMLgravity model estimators

This study examines whether trade gains from U.S. free trade agreements (FTAs) depend on the depth of tariff liberalization achieved, using bilateral trade flows with 20 FTA partners from 2000 to 2024. Grounded in the gravity model and trade creation framework, a panel regression is estimated via Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE), with the Poisson Pseudo Maximum Likelihood (PPML) estimator serving as the primary robustness check, addressing heteroskedasticity and preserving the gravity model's multiplicative structure. Both estimators use the World Integrated Trade Solution trade-weighted average preferential tariff, UN Comtrade trade data, and World Bank macroeconomic indicators, with standard errors clustered by country. FTA entry is associated with approximately 24 percent higher total trade and 27 percent higher exports under TWFE. The tariff–FTA interaction is negative and significant across both estimators: at the 1 percent level under TWFE, and at the 5 percent level (total trade) and 1 percent level (imports) under PPML, confirming that the central finding is robust across estimators. A one percentage point increase in residual tariffs within an FTA is associated with 5.2 to 8.7 percent lower trade flows. These findings demonstrate that trade gains from FTAs are proportional to the depth of tariff liberalization actually achieved.

A National Spot Price Database for U.S. Grain Elevators: Corn, Soybeans & Wheat, 2004–2025

M. Datta · Advisor: Dr. Jason Grant

Virginia Tech, Department of Agricultural and Applied Economics

~3,000U.S. grain elevators
20M+price observations
2004–2025time coverage
3commodities: corn, soybeans, wheat

This dissertation chapter constructs a novel, high-frequency spot price dataset spanning approximately 3,000 grain elevators across the United States for corn, soybeans, and wheat, comprising more than 20 million observations from 2004 to 2025. The resulting panel offers spatial and temporal granularity well beyond existing county- or state-level price series, providing a foundation for studying local price formation, basis behavior, and spatial market integration in U.S. grain markets. This page will be updated with the full working paper and data documentation as the project progresses.
