Research

Trade policy, agricultural markets & applied econometrics

My research sits at the intersection of international trade policy and agricultural economics: tariffs, free trade agreements, and non-tariff measures on the trade-policy side, and spatial price effects in U.S. grain markets on the agricultural side, using panel econometrics, event-study, and difference-in-differences methods throughout.

The Impact of U.S. Tariffs and Free Trade Agreements on Bilateral Trade

Chowdhury, M. Datta, and D. Miljkovic

Submitted to The Manchester School, 2026

+24%trade with FTA entry (TWFE)
20U.S. FTA partner countries
2000–2024panel coverage
TWFE + PPMLgravity model estimators

This study examines whether trade gains from U.S. free trade agreements (FTAs) depend on the depth of tariff liberalization achieved, using bilateral trade flows with 20 FTA partners from 2000 to 2024. Grounded in the gravity model and trade creation framework, a panel regression is estimated via Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE), with the Poisson Pseudo Maximum Likelihood (PPML) estimator serving as the primary robustness check, addressing heteroskedasticity and preserving the gravity model's multiplicative structure. Both estimators use the World Integrated Trade Solution trade-weighted average preferential tariff, UN Comtrade trade data, and World Bank macroeconomic indicators, with standard errors clustered by country. FTA entry is associated with approximately 24 percent higher total trade and 27 percent higher exports under TWFE. The tariff–FTA interaction is negative and significant across both estimators: at the 1 percent level under TWFE, and at the 5 percent level (total trade) and 1 percent level (imports) under PPML, confirming that the central finding is robust across estimators. A one percentage point increase in residual tariffs within an FTA is associated with 5.2 to 8.7 percent lower trade flows. These findings demonstrate that trade gains from FTAs are proportional to the depth of tariff liberalization actually achieved.

Sanitary and Phytosanitary Measures, Technical Barriers to Trade, and U.S. Agricultural Exports

with Dr. Jason Grant and Emre Karagulle

Virginia Tech, Department of Agricultural and Applied Economics

This project examines sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) measures and technical barriers to trade (TBT) affecting U.S. agricultural exports. We are extending an existing database of barriers identified in USTR's National Trade Estimate reports beyond 2021 and exploring how exports adjust over time following the introduction and removal of these measures. The project also considers modern event-study and staggered difference-in-differences approaches to study exporter adaptation and post-barrier recovery.

The Impact of Processing Plant Entry on U.S. Local Grain Prices: Evidence from Soybean Crush and Corn Ethanol Plants

M. Datta · Advisor: Dr. Sandro Steinbach

North Dakota State University, Department of Agribusiness and Applied Economics

+6.9¢/bucorn price effect, within 25 miles
2004–2025daily elevator-level price data
$495Mmodeled 2022 soybean farmer revenue gain

This thesis examines how the entry of soybean crushing plants and corn ethanol plants affects local grain prices in the United States. Using daily elevator-level spot prices from 2004 to 2025, plant-opening information, and spatial data, I estimate price effects using difference-in-differences, event-study, and continuous distance-decay models. The results show that processing-plant entry raises nearby grain prices, with the strongest effects concentrated close to the plant. Soybean crush plant entry increases soybean prices by about 7.1 cents per bushel within 25 miles and 6.4 cents within 50 miles, while ethanol plant entry raises corn prices by about 6.9 cents within 25 miles and 3.2 cents within 50 miles. A capacity-weighted spatial model shows that these effects decline continuously with distance and are stronger around medium and large plants. The analysis also documents regional heterogeneity and translates the estimated price effects into model-implied farmer revenue gains. For soybean, these gains rise from roughly $135 million in 2004 to nearly $495 million in 2022. The thesis concludes by applying the estimated spatial model to five announced soybean-processing plants, illustrating how future processing investment may affect nearby grain markets.
